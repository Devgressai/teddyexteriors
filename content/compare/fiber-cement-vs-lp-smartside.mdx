## Both are good products — they're not interchangeable

Fiber cement (represented in this market mainly by James Hardie) and LP SmartSide (an engineered-wood product from Louisiana-Pacific) are the two systems most Pacific Northwest exterior contractors install at scale. They solve the same problem — durable, paintable, long-service-life exterior cladding — but they go about it differently.

## Composition

- **Fiber cement** is a cement-and-cellulose composite. Non-combustible classification. Heavier per square foot than engineered wood.
- **LP SmartSide** is a resin-treated engineered-wood product (OSB-based, treated with a zinc-borate preservative). Combustible classification but with wind, moisture, and impact resistance tuned by the resin treatment.

For current manufacturer specifications, warranty terms, and installation requirements, consult the manufacturer directly — the links at the end of this page lead to primary sources. Specifications change over time; do not treat competitor websites or contractor marketing as authoritative.

## Installation handling

| Topic | Fiber cement | LP SmartSide |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting | Dust is a respiratory hazard — specific saws and shrouded blades are required. | Standard carbide-tipped saws; dust is wood dust, which is still a health risk. |
| Weight on the wall | Noticeably heavier; affects fastening density and some scaffolding/handling. | Lighter; faster to handle on long runs. |
| Fastener options | Nail or screw; manufacturer specifies in-field and seam fastening density. | Nail; blind-nail or face-nail per manufacturer guidance. |
| Expansion/contraction | Minimal relative to wood. | Dimensionally stable, but gaps and sealant locations still matter. |

Both systems require correct WRB and flashing underneath. Neither tolerates poor flashing just because the cladding is engineered.

## Appearance

- **Fiber cement** holds crisp straight lines; paint adheres well; profiles include lap, panel, shingle, and board-and-batten styles. Finish options include prefinished color and primed-for-field-paint.
- **LP SmartSide** has a pronounced woodgrain texture by default (and a smooth option). Longer board lengths reduce the number of visible butt joints on long walls. Prefinished and field-painted options.

Both can look excellent on a Pacific Northwest home. The right choice usually turns on the specific house, the preferred finish character, and the trim strategy.

## Maintenance

- **Fiber cement** typically needs repainting on a cycle driven by paint quality and exposure, not the siding itself. Caulk at joints and penetrations is the item that gets replaced before the siding does.
- **LP SmartSide** also runs on a repaint cycle. The resin treatment handles moisture well in service, but exposed cut ends should be sealed per manufacturer guidance.

Neither product is "paint for life." Treat anyone claiming otherwise with caution.

## Service life and warranty

Both systems carry substantial manufacturer warranties with specific exclusions. Workmanship warranties — covering how the installation was performed — are separate and come from the contractor, not the manufacturer. Read both.

## How we think about it

If a homeowner's design direction leans crisp and painted with distinct trim relationships, fiber cement is usually a strong fit. If the design leans toward a visible woodgrain character with longer uninterrupted runs, LP SmartSide is usually a strong fit. The envelope detailing — WRB, flashing, sealants, dissimilar-material transitions — matters more than which cladding is on the wall. Both products fail in the same ways when the layers behind them are wrong.
